# Troop 23 Basecamp

The private troop app for Scouts BSA Troop 23, Tallahassee, Florida.

## Signing in

Everyone on the TroopWebHost roster has a **personal code** (like `K7QM-2XPA`) and a sign-in link (`…/#/join/K7QM-2XPA`). Opening the link, or typing the code, signs that phone in as that person. Their sign-ups, packing checklists, chat and advancement then follow them to any phone they sign in on. Leaders send codes from Leader tools → Members (**Send invite** on one person, or **Invite list (CSV)** for everyone). Parents get linked to their scouts there too, so one parent can sign the whole family up.

The troop code still works as a **guest** sign-in (no chat, nothing synced). Adults marked Leader get leader mode automatically, with no passcode.

## What's in it

- **Outings.** Campouts, hikes, paddles, service and meetings, with dates, meeting place, cost and the plan. Everyone taps Going / Maybe / Can't go, and the outing shows who's coming by patrol.
- **Patrol meal planning** for each outing. Meal slots fill in from the outing dates. Pick recipes or write your own, and quantities scale to the patrol's sign-ups. The shopping list builds itself, combining ingredients across meals, with checkboxes for the store and a note when the troop pantry already has something. Plus a food budget with receipts and cost per person, and a duty roster that rotates cook, assistant cook, fire & water and cleanup across every meal.
- **Troop kitchen.** A recipe library (27 camp classics to start, scouts add their own) with a servings scaler, and the troop pantry: staples by location, with "running low" flags.
- **Gear locker.** Everything the troop owns, with tag numbers, condition and location. Check items out to a person, patrol and outing, then check them back in and flag anything broken. Each item keeps a full history. From an outing, check out a whole trip's gear at once and check it all back in after.
- **Packing lists.** The standard overnight list plus the leader's extras for each trip, checked off on the phone.
- **Photo gallery.** Browse by event (cover cards by year) or as one wall; filter by year, kind of outing, patrol, or search events, places, captions and who's tagged. Heart favorites (synced to your account), pick photos to play or save, and build a slideshow from any events, years, patrols or favorites, with event title cards, captions, speed and shuffle. Includes 1,000 photos from the TroopWebHost gallery (2022 on), each filed under its event.
- **Photos.** The Pack 6 Photo Trail system: snap or upload, every outing is its own album, tag scouts, and location data is stripped on the phone. Leaders review every photo against each family's photo permission (Public OK / Troop only / No photos). There's also a full-screen slideshow for Courts of Honor.
- **Bulletin.** Leader announcements on the Home screen, with pinning.
- **Chat**, built for Safeguarding Youth Training rules. Conversations open full screen. The message box grows as you type. In conversations (not boards), **Full message** opens an email-style writer with a subject line, a big writing area and a toolbar: bold, italic, underline, strikethrough, headings, bulleted and numbered lists, quotes, indent, highlight, text colors, links, divider lines and undo. Quick messages support **bold**, _italic_, ~~strike~~, lists and links (tap Aa), and up to 10 photos or files each (photos are resized on the phone with location data removed; PDFs, Word, Excel, PowerPoint and text files up to 4 MB). When a leader hides a reported message, its photos and files are hidden too. The Scoutmaster and Committee Chair are automatically in every conversation, so there's never a one-on-one chat. Messages can't be deleted, and anyone can report one for leaders to review. Boards: whole troop, Patrol Leaders' Council, each patrol, adult leaders, and the troop's groups (from the TroopWebHost e-mail groups). Scouts also get **Ask a leader**: request a board of review or a Scoutmaster conference, which opens a conversation with the right adults plus the two-deep leaders.
- **Never miss a message.** A bell in the header and a pulsing Chat badge count conversations that need you. Home opens with a "waiting on you" card, and the first visit each session shows what came in. A conversation needs a reply when someone else spoke last (or named you on a board) in the last three weeks.
- **Writing helper (✨ in Chat).** Suggests a clearer, kinder, complete version of a message and lists anything the other person asked that the draft doesn't answer. It never sends anything. It runs on Cloudflare Workers AI's free daily allowance (Llama 3.1 8B, roughly 1,000 suggestions a day; 40 per person) and never uses Netlify AI credits. Set `CF_ACCOUNT_ID` and `CF_AI_TOKEN` (a Cloudflare API token with Workers AI permission) in Netlify's environment variables. Without them, or once the free allowance runs out for the day, it gives built-in writing tips. Leaders can turn it off in Leader tools → Settings.
- **Hikes (like Strava).** Tap Record at the trailhead and Basecamp tracks distance, time, pace and feet climbed on a live, full-screen trail map with the numbers floating over it. Pick who's hiking, so everyone in the group gets credit, and tie it to an outing. Pause and resume without counting the drive in between. If the app is closed mid-hike, it picks up where it left off. When the hike is saved, the climbing is checked against map elevation data (Copernicus DEM via the free Open-Meteo service), which is more accurate than phone GPS. The first and last 200 m are left off the saved map by default, so a hike that starts at home doesn't show the house. Each hike's page opens on a big map with the title and stats over it, the route colored by elevation or pace, mile markers, start and finish flags, full screen, and an elevation chart you can slide along to see the spot on the map. Maps: Topo, Satellite, Hybrid (satellite with labels), USGS Topo and Street, with Hiking trails and Hillshade overlays. Satellite, Hybrid, USGS Topo and Hillshade come from USGS (public domain). Topo is OpenTopoMap, Street is OpenStreetMap and the trails overlay is Waymarked Trails, each credited on the map. Hikes can also be logged by hand. The Hikes page has four views: **Troop** and **Mine** (feed cards with a real map preview of each route, distance, climb, time and an elevation sparkline), **Map** (every hike on one map, one color each; tap a trail or pick it from the list to zoom in), and **Stats** (troop miles by month, top hikers and patrols). Each hike's page adds a mile-by-mile breakdown (pace bars and climb or drop per mile) and photos shared from the same outing. Hikes are visible only to signed-in members, and the recorder or a leader can edit or delete one. **Turning on location** is one button (More, Hikes, and the recorder). It first shows what the phone's question will look like and which button to tap, then asks the phone, and if the phone says no, Basecamp works out which browser it's in and shows step-by-step help for that browser: on iPhone, Safari, Chrome, the Google app, Edge, Firefox, DuckDuckGo, Brave, Opera or the Home Screen app; on Android, Chrome, Samsung Internet, Firefox, Edge, Opera, Brave, DuckDuckGo, the Google app or the home-screen app. Links opened inside texts, email or social apps are spotted too, with a one-tap Copy link to move to Safari or Chrome. People can correct the browser if it guessed wrong, "Reload and try again" reloads and asks again in one tap, and "Details for a leader" shows (and copies) exactly what the phone reported. The help goes one step at a time, with a little animated phone showing each screen and the exact row to tap, plus the exact taps in words then checks again on its own when you come back. Phones don't let websites open the Settings app themselves, so the help shows exactly where to go. Keep the app open while recording: phones pause location when the screen locks, so the screen stays on.
- **Games (More → Games, and on Home).** Practice for rank advancement with 141 original questions in 12 topics (Oath & Law, knots, first aid, camping, cooking, map & compass, nature, Leave No Trace, safety & swimming, citizenship, patrol leadership, trail to Eagle), each tagged by rank from Scout to Star–Eagle. Filter by rank and topic, then play **Quiz Trail** (10 questions, an explanation after each, streak bonus), **Speed Round** (60 seconds, as many as you can), or **Puzzles** (put steps in order, match pairs, unscramble words). The **Daily Challenge** is 5 questions, the same for everyone that day, once a day, with a day streak. **Campfire Clash** is a live game for meetings: a leader taps Host a game, puts the 4-letter code on a TV or projector, and everyone joins on their phone. Faster right answers score more, and it can run patrol vs. patrol (patrols ranked by average score). Scouts earn XP, levels (Camp Rookie to Legend of the Trail) and 10 badges, and see topic mastery, plus weekly, daily, patrol and all-time leaderboards. Every answer is checked on the server, so scores can't be faked. Sound and buzz can be muted. Advancement's "On the way to…" card links to practice for that rank. The questions are written for Basecamp (not copied from the Scout Handbook) and are practice only — leaders and counselors still sign off on requirements. Questions live in `troop/quiz.js`; add or edit them there.
- **Uniform inspection (More → Uniform inspection; patrol leaders also get a button on Home).** The patrol leader marks who's here, then goes scout by scout on a cartoon uniform with a pin on each part: shirt, neckerchief, shoulder loops, council patch and numerals, U.S. flag, patrol patch, position patch, rank patch, World Crest, pants, belt, socks, shoes (and the hat, if the troop wants it). Everything starts as Sharp, so a good uniform is one tap; tap a pin (or the ✓ ~ ✗ buttons in the list) to mark Needs a fix (half points) or Missing (none). The position patch can be marked "doesn't apply". The score out of 100 and the cartoon's face change as you go, and parts that are missing fade out. At the end it shows the patrol average with ratings from Room to Grow to Inspection Legend. The **Patrol Cup** ranks patrols by this month's average. Patrol leaders and assistant patrol leaders inspect their own patrol; the SPL, ASPL, troop guides and adult leaders can inspect any patrol. Each scout (and their parents) sees their own result and what to fix; everyone sees patrol averages. Leaders can turn the hat and neckerchief on or off for the troop. Scores are worked out on the server.
- **Troop elections (More → Troop elections, and a Vote now banner on Home while voting is open).** A leader sets up the ballot: positions: every Scouts BSA youth position of responsibility, named the way the troop names them (Senior Patrol Leader, Assistant Senior Patrol Leader, Troop Guide, Quartermaster, Assistant Quartermaster, Scribe, Historian, Librarian, Instructor, Chaplain's Aide, Den Chief, Junior Assistant Scoutmaster, Order of the Arrow Representative, Outdoor Ethics Guide, Bugler, Scout Webmaster), with **Add all** for the whole list, or type your own. **Patrol Leader**, **Assistant Patrol Leader** and **Patrol Quartermaster** add one race per patrol, and only that patrol votes in it. A draft ballot can have positions with nobody running yet; voting opens once every position has at least one candidate and who's running. Scouts tap one name per position and cast their vote once. Ballots are secret: Basecamp keeps who has voted (for turnout) but never shows who voted for whom. **TV screen** shows a scan-to-vote QR code, turnout, and live vote bars with a crown on whoever is ahead, or just turnout with a big reveal when voting closes ("Show live results on the TV"). When the leader closes voting, the screen shows the winners with confetti (ties are flagged for leaders to decide). **Vote on this phone** lets a leader walk around with their phone for scouts who don't have one: pick the scout, hand over the phone, the scout votes privately, and the phone shows a hand-it-back screen. Scouts who already voted are greyed out, and each scout gets one vote no matter which phone it's cast on.
- **Meeting notes (Home tile, More → Patrols & PLC → Meeting notes; leaders also get More → Leaders → Committee minutes and a shortcut in Leader tools).** Two notebooks. **PLC notes** are for scouts and leaders: any scout can read them, and the Patrol Leaders' Council (SPL, ASPL, patrol leaders and assistants, troop guides, scribe) and leaders can write them. **Committee minutes** are for leaders only: no scout or parent can open them or their files. Each set of notes has the meeting, date, who was there, the notes (bold, lists and links; a PLC or committee template to start from), and up to 30 photos or files (whiteboard pictures, agendas, budgets, PDF, Word, Excel, PowerPoint or text, up to 4 MB each; photos are shrunk on the phone first). Committee minutes start as a Draft; after the committee approves them a leader taps **Mark approved**, which locks them (a leader can mark them as a draft again to fix something). PLC notes can be changed by whoever wrote them or a leader. Search and year-by-year lists make old notes easy to find. Parents don't see either notebook.
- **Campfire book (Home tile, More → Scouting → Campfire book).** Songs, skits, run-ons, cheers, Scoutmaster's Minutes and patrol games in one searchable book. **Songs:** full words for public-domain songs (published before 1929: Clementine, Home on the Range, Railroad, She'll Be Coming 'Round the Mountain, Shenandoah, Auld Lang Syne, America the Beautiful, rounds and more), 8 new repeat-after-me and action songs written for Basecamp (The Raccoon Raid, The S'more Song, Leave No Trace Cadence, Patrol on Parade, The Compass Song, The Dutch Oven Shuffle, The Bowline Song, The Mosquito Waltz), plus camp favorites (Purple Stew, Boom Chicka Boom, Princess Pat, The Other Day I Met a Bear, Scout Vespers and others) listed with how they go. Their words may still be under copyright, so Basecamp doesn't print them; a leader or the PLC can add the words your troop sings, saved for the troop only. **Skits** (15) are written for Basecamp with cast size, time, props and a script; **run-ons** (14), **cheers** (15), **Scoutmaster's Minutes** (6), and **patrol games** (15) with what you need, how to play, variations and safety notes. **Kim's Game** plays on a phone at three levels. Every page has **Fire light** (dark, warm text that's easy on night vision) and **Bigger text**. Leaders and the Patrol Leaders' Council star troop favorites, add their own songs, skits and the rest, and plan campfires; other scouts and parents can suggest things for a leader to approve (switch this off in Settings). Leaders can hide anything the troop doesn't use. **Campfire plans:** name and date, then **Fill it for me** builds a lineup that follows the fire (lively opening, skits and silly songs at the peak, quiet songs, a Scoutmaster's Minute and a closing song), troop favorites first. Assign a patrol or scout to each act, reorder, add from the book or add anything else. **Emcee mode** runs the show full screen: one act at a time with the words or notes, who's up next, a cheer to lead after each act, and the screen kept on. The next campfire shows at the top of the book. Campfire rules (keep it kind, no put-downs, no fire stunts) are on the Skits tab.
- **Ceremonies (Home tile, More → Scouting → Ceremonies).** Scripts for the opening and closing flag ceremonies (indoors), morning and evening colors at a flagpole, flying the flag at half-staff, folding the flag (with a 5-step drawing), retiring a worn flag (with safety notes, including not burning nylon or polyester flags), and a Scout Law candle ceremony for Courts of Honor. Each lists who and what you need, then every step with who says what and what everyone does. **Lead it** walks the caller through it in big print, one step per tap, with the Pledge on screen and the phone kept awake. Plus quick reference pages for ceremony commands and flag etiquette (from the U.S. Flag Code, 4 U.S.C. §§ 4–10). Troops can add their own ceremonies (crossovers, Eagle courts, a flag-fold reading) by typing one step per line ("Caller: Troop, attention.").
- **Knot trainer (More → Scouting → Knot trainer).** Every knot and lashing in the Scout through First Class requirements (square knot, two half hitches, taut-line hitch, sheet bend, bowline, clove hitch, timber hitch, square, shear and diagonal lashings, whipping and fusing) plus the figure eight, each with the requirement number, what it's for, numbered steps written for Basecamp, a saying to remember it, how to check it, and a "Watch it tied" link (Scout Life or Scout Pioneering). **Practice step by step** shows one big step at a time while you hold a rope. **Tie it!** is a stopwatch: tie the knot, have a buddy or leader check it, and save your time; each knot has a troop board of the fastest checked times (first name and last initial). Scouts can mark knots they can tie, and the page shows how many of the 11 rank knots and lashings they have. **Which knot?** is an 8-question quiz on picking the right knot for the job.
- **Patrol points (More → Patrols & PLC → Patrol points, and on Home).** A running patrol competition. Leaders, the SPL and the ASPL give or take points with a reason (quick buttons for +1 to +25 and common reasons). Uniform inspections add the patrol's score divided by ten, and approved scavenger hunt photos add their points (each can be switched off in Settings). **Show on a TV** puts big live standings with a crown on a screen. Leaders can start a new season (older points stop counting; nothing is deleted).
- **Scavenger hunts (More → Patrols & PLC → Scavenger hunts).** A leader makes a list (with 15 ideas to tap in) and points for each item, then opens the hunt; Home shows a banner while it's open. Scouts tap **Snap it** on an item to send a photo for their patrol (resized on the phone, location removed). Leaders approve or reject each photo; standings update as they go, and approved finds count toward patrol points. Hunt photos stay inside the hunt: only leaders and the scout's own patrol can see them.
- **Campsite duty roster (each outing's Duties tab, and on Home during the outing).** Troop jobs (color guard, firewood and fire ring, water, campsite cleanup, campfire program, or your own list in Settings) for each day of the outing, rotated fairly across patrols, with optional names. Leaders and the PLC rotate, shuffle, change and save it. While the outing is on, Home shows each patrol its jobs for the day. (Patrol cooking duties stay on the Meals tab.)
- **Night sky (More → Scouting → Night sky, a link on each overnight outing, and on Home during a campout).** A star chart for the troop's hometown, the outing's campsite, or wherever the phone is, at any time from 5 PM to 5 AM: stars, constellation lines and names, bright star names, planets and the Moon, turned to the direction you're facing. Sunset, full darkness, the Moon's phase and moonrise; which planets are up and where to look; easy finds right now (Big Dipper and Polaris, Cassiopeia, Summer Triangle, Orion, Sirius, the Pleiades, Scorpius, Leo, Arcturus, the Great Square, the Andromeda Galaxy) with directions; and stargazing tips. **Night vision** turns the page dim red. **Point your phone at the sky** works like a stargazing app: hold the phone up and the sky moves with it, using the phone's compass and tilt (the phone asks to allow motion once), with the camera behind the stars if you like and the name of whatever is in the middle. Drag sideways to line it up if the phone's compass is off, or drag to look around on a computer. Star data from d3-celestial (BSD license, Olaf Frohn) and sky math from Astronomy Engine (MIT license, Don Cross).
- **Court of Honor planner (leaders: More → Leaders → Court of Honor planner).** Starts with the usual agenda (opening flag ceremony, welcome, Oath and Law, rank advancements, merit badges, special recognition, Scoutmaster's Minute, thank-yous, closing ceremony), each linked to its ceremony script, with who leads each part; reorder or add items. **Bring in what's earned** lists every rank and merit badge in scouts' advancement records with no presented date (last six months by default), scout by scout, to tick into the awards list; add special awards by hand. Tick awards as they're presented. **Program** makes a printable program (or PDF from the print screen) with the agenda, rank advancements by rank, and merit badges by scout, with full names or first name and last initial. **Awards list for Scoutbook** downloads a CSV for the Advancement Chair.
- **Advancement next steps.** Each scout's page (and Home, for scouts) suggests what to work on next, from their own record: what's left for the next rank (merit badge counts, Eagle-required badges, time in rank), partial badges closest to done with their counselor, Eagle-required badges to start (preferring ones with a troop counselor or an upcoming outing that covers them), upcoming outings that fit badges they haven't earned, camping nights toward the Camping merit badge (20 nights), and hiking. Leaders get **Troop planning** under Advancement: the Eagle-required badges the most scouts still need (merit badge class ideas, with counselors), who has the merit badges done for the next rank, who is close to their next rank, and partial badges that are nearly done. Rules used: Star 6 badges (4 Eagle-required), Life 11 (7), Eagle 21 including all 13 Eagle-required (Citizenship in Society was discontinued Feb 27, 2026). These are suggestions; the Scoutmaster, counselors and Scoutbook have the final say.
- **Advancement.** Each scout's rank ladder, next-rank requirements, Eagle-required badges, badges earned and in progress, and activity totals, imported from TroopWebHost. Scouts see their own record, parents see their linked scouts', leaders see everyone.
- **Recipe scanner.** Photograph a recipe card or cookbook page. The phone reads it on the device, breaks out the ingredients and steps, scales it to the number of people going, and estimates the cost.
- **Notifications.** Signed-in members turn them on per phone under More → Notifications, and choose messages, boards and announcements. On iPhone, Basecamp has to be added to the Home Screen first (iOS 16.4 or later). Notification keys are made automatically, so there's nothing to set up.
- **Outings calendar.** Outings → Calendar shows a month grid colored by kind of outing; swipe or jump to any month and year, tap a day for its events. Past outings can be filtered by year, kind and search, with photo counts.
- **Troop calendar.** Outings → "Put troop outings in your phone's calendar" subscribes Apple, Google or Outlook calendars to a live feed. Each outing also has Add to calendar.
- **Food budget calculator** and a shopping-list price book: per-meal rates from `troop.mealCosts`, headcount from sign-ups, the amount to collect per scout, and prices remembered from past trips.

## Getting around

Back buttons say where they go ("‹ Outings", "‹ Hikes") and go back to the last *page*, not the last tap. Switching tabs or filters inside a page (an outing's Meals/Gear/Packing, Kitchen tabs, photo filters) doesn't add a step, so Back — and the phone's back gesture — skip straight to the section you came from. Opened from a link or a notification, Back goes to the page's own section instead of leaving the app.

## Phones, tablets and computers

Checked on screens from a 320-pixel-wide phone (iPhone SE size) through phones, tablets and laptops up to a 1920-pixel TV, and with phones turned sideways. Small phones get a compact header (the Leader badge becomes a shield icon); sideways phones get a slimmer tab bar and a header that scrolls away; typing boxes use 16-pixel text so iPhones don't zoom in when you tap them.


On phones, the tabs are along the bottom. On computers (1000 px and wider) they move to a sidebar on the left, Home shows two columns, and rows of patrols, filters and tabs wrap onto extra lines so everything is reachable with a mouse.

## Leader tools (More → Leader tools)

Signing in with the leader passcode turns on leader mode for that browser tab. Leaders can then add and edit outings and delete gear or recipes. A **Jump to** row at the top goes straight to Committee minutes, PLC notes, Elections, Inspections and Troop planning. The leader tools also have:

- **Members.** Personal codes, invites, the Leader switch, parent ↔ scout links, and the two adults copied on every chat.
- **Link families** (in Members). Suggests parent–scout pairs who share a last name, to confirm all at once.
- **Troop setup.** Merit badge counselors (who counsels which badge; scouts find them under Advancement → Counselors and can message troop counselors), leadership positions for each member, places outings go (typing one in an outing fills in the address, phone, website and map pin), and shortcuts to everything else that came from TroopWebHost.
- **Groups.** The troop's groups as chat boards or "Ask a leader" requests. Add or remove people any time.
- **Chat reports.** Hide or keep reported messages.
- **Photos.** Review uploads against photo permission.
- **Permission.** The family roster, including the No-photos list.
- **Bulletin.** Post and pin announcements.
- **Settings** (each section opens with a tap, so the page stays short):
  - **Features.** Switch off any part of Basecamp the troop doesn't use: Hikes, Games, Uniform inspection, Troop elections, Meeting notes, Campfire book, Ceremonies, Knot trainer, Patrol points, Scavenger hunts, Campsite duty roster, Night sky, Court of Honor planner, Troop kitchen, Gear locker, Merit badge counselors. A switched-off part disappears from Home and More for everyone and is turned off on the server too; leaders can still open it, with a note that it's off. Nothing is deleted, and switching it back on brings everything back. Options under each: hide the start and finish of new hikes by default; Campfire Clash live games on or off; which optional uniform items are checked (hat, neckerchief); live election results on the TV by default; whether parents can read PLC notes; whether scouts and parents can suggest songs and skits for the campfire book; whether inspections and scavenger hunts add patrol points; and the troop jobs each duty roster starts with.
  - **Patrols**, **Photo albums**, **Standard packing list** (the everyday list every scout sees; one item per line, groups you can add or remove), and the **Chat writing helper**.
- **Records.** CSV downloads, grouped: roster (no codes) and photo permission; outing sign-ups, food spending, gear inventory, gear history and hikes; uniform inspections, election results (vote counts only, never who voted for whom) and games; and the photo log. They download straight to the phone or computer, whether you signed in with your personal code or the leader passcode.

## Sync center (Leader tools → Sync)

Scoutbook Plus, TroopWebHost and Black Pug (Scouting Event) don't offer connections for other apps (no API), so records move between them as the CSV files each one already exports. The Sync center makes that a routine instead of a chore:

- **Who can use it:** leaders whose position is Scoutmaster, Committee Chair, Advancement Chair (Committee Advancement Chair / Advancement Coordinator) or Webmaster, plus anyone with the leader passcode. Change the list in Settings → Sync center.
- **Status board:** when each source was last brought in and by whom (green under a month, amber under three months, red after that).
- **A checklist for each role** with the exact menu path in the other system:
  - *Advancement Chair:* TroopWebHost → Export to Scoutbook Plus for new awards; Scoutbook Plus → Reports → Quick Export → Advancement, dropped here to compare with TroopWebHost (and uploaded in TroopWebHost); Black Pug's "Scoutbook Export" after summer camp.
  - *Committee Chair:* Scoutbook Plus roster report → TroopWebHost, and dropped here to see who's missing or not registered; after elections, one tap updates positions in Basecamp, plus a results download to enter in Scoutbook and TroopWebHost; switching off people who have left.
  - *Webmaster:* the monthly TroopWebHost export bundle (Active Roster, Events, Event Participation, Rank Advancement History, Merit Badge History, Merit Badges In Progress, Rank Requirements Earned, Inventory Items, Checked Out Items, E-Mail Groups, Event Locations, User Permissions, Leadership Positions), the calendar link, and the activity log.
  - *Scoutmaster:* the status board, the differences list, and Troop planning.
- **Bring files in:** drop any mix of files; Basecamp recognizes each one by its columns. TroopWebHost files update people, patrols, positions, advancement, attendance, gear, chat groups, outings, counselors and places (outings a leader edited in Basecamp, counselors and places are left alone) and list anyone no longer on the roster so a leader can switch them off. A Scoutbook advancement file is compared with TroopWebHost: only in Scoutbook, only in TroopWebHost, and done-but-not-awarded, scout by scout. A Black Pug file saves camp results, which scouts and parents also see on the scout's Advancement page. A Scoutbook roster report is checked against Basecamp.
- **Privacy:** files are read on the leader's device. Only names, patrols, positions and advancement are sent; BSA member IDs, contact details, birthdays, medical and money never leave the device, and files with passwords are refused.
- **Send records out:** activity log (outings attended and hikes recorded in Basecamp, with camping nights and hiking miles), election results, and the Basecamp roster.
- **Sync history:** every import, compare and change, with who did it.

The command-line importers in `scripts/` still work, but the Sync center does the same job from a phone or computer.

## Settings

Edit `troop/troop.js` for names, colors, outing types, gear categories, duty roles, the packing list, starter recipes, sharing rules and photo-permission wording. Have the Scoutmaster and chartered organization review the permission wording before rollout.

**Official artwork.** Put the troop's licensed Scouting America artwork in `public/brand/` and set `logo` (and `logoOnDark` for a white version) in `troop/troop.js`. The header, sign-in screen and slideshow use it automatically. Until then the app uses its "Troop 23" lettering mark.

Secrets go in Netlify → Site configuration → Environment variables:

| Variable | What it does |
|---|---|
| `TROOP_CODE` | The code everyone types to open the app. Not case-sensitive. Change it each year. |
| `LEADER_PASSCODE` | Opens leader tools and leader mode. |
| `CF_ACCOUNT_ID` | Cloudflare account ID for the free chat writing helper (optional). |
| `CF_AI_TOKEN` | Cloudflare API token with Workers AI permission (optional, secret). |

Data lives in Netlify Blobs, in a store named after `troop.id`. No database to set up.

## Importing from TroopWebHost

TroopWebHost has no way for another app to read it, so exports come over as CSV files (TroopWebHost → Reports → export to Excel). Put them in one folder, then run:

```
python3 scripts/import-troopwebhost.py <Export_Events.csv> <site-url> <leader-passcode>
python3 scripts/import-troopwebhost-all.py <exports-folder> <site-url> <leader-passcode>
python3 scripts/import-locations.py <Maintain_Event_Locations.csv> <site-url> <leader-passcode>
```

- **Events** → outings (re-running updates the same outings).
- **Everything else** reads the active roster, user permissions, leadership positions, rank and merit badge history, merit badges in progress, rank requirements, event participation, inventory and checked-out items, and e-mail groups. It brings over names, adult/scout, patrol, positions, rank, advancement, attendance, gear and groups. **Contact info, birthdays, ages, BSA IDs, medical, allergies, money and addresses are never read.** Re-running keeps everyone's personal code, leader changes, parent links and group edits.
- **Photos** come from TroopWebHost → Photo Gallery → Download Photos, gathered in a signed-in browser. `scripts/import-twh-photo-events.py` makes a past outing for each event so photos land in the right album. Imported photos wait in Leader tools → Photos for review (filter by album, approve all shown) before families see them.
- **Counselors and places**: `python3 scripts/import-twh-extras.py <exports-folder> <site-url> <leader-passcode>` adds merit badge counselors (from Merit Badges In Progress and the Merit Badge Counselor role) and every event location. Existing entries are kept.
- **Locations** add the address, phone, website and map pin to outings by location name.

Add `--dry` to see what would change without sending anything.

## Run locally

```
npm install
npm run dev
```

Local troop code: `troop23`. Local leader passcode: `leader`. Data saves to `.data/`. Run `python3 scripts/e2e.py <folder>` (on an empty `.data`) for a full guest walkthrough with screenshots. After importing the TroopWebHost exports locally, `scripts/e2e-accounts.py` and `scripts/e2e-groups.py` walk through accounts, chat, advancement, the scanner, budgets and groups.
